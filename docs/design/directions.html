  • Desktop was broken, not just plain. The product image had no height limit and the two-column breakpoint sat at 900 px, so in a narrower frame it collapsed to one column and you scrolled through a single photo. The image is now capped at 76 vh — one shot never exceeds one screen — and the split happens at 860 px, with the thumbnails on a vertical rail beside it.
  • The type was thin because it was tech-sans. My first shortlist was drawn entirely from modern grotesques, which is why all three read sharp and cheap. Screening editorial faces found Playfair Display — a high-contrast fashion serif that actually contains U+02BB. Prata, Forum, Tenor Sans, Bodoni Moda and Marcellus — the usual Cyrillic fashion faces — all fail it.
  • Light text on dark reads thinner than it measures. Body weight is now 500, never 400, and the text is warm off-white #EFE9DE rather than pure white, which stops the halation that makes white-on-black look brittle.
  • Dark, not black. The ground is a warm charcoal with three layered surfaces above it, so panels separate by material instead of by outline. Brass carries the price, the chosen size and the buy button — nothing else.

Still a mockup, not a photograph

The garments are drawn. Layout, hierarchy and type are real; how good the shop looks still depends on the photography that replaces them.
